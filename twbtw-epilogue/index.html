✦   The Wild Beyond the Witchlight   ✦
Epilogue
A story of debts, departures & distant roads
I  ·  The Closing Gate
The gates of the Palace of Heart's Desire close behind them for the last time. The four of them — Rumpelstiltskin, Seabert, Therai, and Silvan — stand in the shimmering air of Prismeer, the sky above Yon bruised violet and gold, the land still trembling back to life after years of stolen time. There are promises left to keep.
II  ·  Small Victories
In the shadows of Motherhorn, they find Hurly — bigger than his reputation, quieter than expected, worn down by Endelyn's false promises and his own stubborn pride. He doesn't say much when they free him. He doesn't have to. Somewhere back at the Witchlight Carnival, his brother Burly is waiting. That's enough. High above the cliffs of Yon, Glister falls into her twin sister's arms — two high elves, reunited at last, their matching laughter ringing out across the same fairgrounds that once knew them both. Small victories. Real ones.
III  ·  The Parting
It is Rumpelstiltskin who notices Therai's eyes first — already somewhere else. Distant. Counting the seconds. Before the dust of Yon has even settled, Therai announces he's leaving. The words land like a door slamming shut. His debt to Prismeer paid just enough to quiet his conscience. His loyalty, it turns out, was never really theirs to begin with. "I have to finish this," he says. Not an apology. Not a goodbye. Silvan shoulders his pack without a word and steps up beside him. The bard and the sorcerer — a pair nonetheless. They step through the planar tear and vanish into a sky of fire and smoke. Somewhere in the roiling streets of the City of Brass, a hag thinks she is safe. She is wrong.
IV  ·  The Road Home
Rumpelstiltskin and Seabert keep their word — every last promise, every last debt — before finally pointing themselves toward the horizon of the material plane. The Feywild fades behind them like a half-remembered dream. They ride in comfortable silence, the same two rogues they've always been. Almost.
Two years well spent.
Here's to every session, every bad roll,
every moment that made it worth showing up.
